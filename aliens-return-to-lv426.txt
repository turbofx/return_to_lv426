Chapter 1.


"Wake up pretty boy!" laughs a marine with a gentle slap to my face.

The cold air hits my bare legs like a wall. This is always the worst part of waking out of hyper sleep.

Tugs and pulls rouse me out of my ethereal slumber as various needles, probes and sensors are pulled out.

I crack my eyes to blazing light.

"Jesus, could you turn the lights up even more?" I grunt back.

"Oh, now you're awake." laughs a voice. I know that voice. My brain is starting to come back online. Corpsman something or other, it'll come to me.

Dickenson is the corpsman. As they go, at least not a total asshole to me. "Chow in twenty. Muster in an hour." replies Dickenson. Apparently she's looked me over and decided to move on.

As my vision adjusts, I lift my head to a satisfying crack as my spine decides it wants to stretch. I look down the line of hyper sleep chambers. This half open, the other half closed. I mentally count, even. No hyper sleep casualties.

The USN DeBrasco is the third ship of the modular gladiator class of star ships and has the latest generation of everything, including the next generation hyper sleep modules. I stagger over to my locker, my muscles protesting with every step.

The black BDU is yet another way I'm a bit different from your standard Colonial Space marine. The collar manages to perfectly rub against my neck right were my neural shunt and the skin meet. I'm convinced the BDU designers designed it this way. Yet another annoyance my brain registers.

"Waking up is always the worst" mumbles a voice next to me. I look over and see Janice. A fellow cyber tech. The clothes are even more ill fitting on her. Her petite size in stark contrast to everything designed for large muscle bound marines. I notice she's donned a scarf around her neck to deal with the collar chaffing issue.

Janice and I have a complicated thing going. She comes over and gives me a hug. I hug back. Ah, no kiss, still complicated.

We wander down the line of lockers collecting the other four members of our rag tag cyber tech gang. All together we head into the chow hall.

The chow hall of this ship is the usual. Polished metal, food you'd rather not eat, created out of who knows what and the usual derisive sneers, outright jeers and the occasional head shake. As usual, the marines have left a table just for us. We get in line with the everybody else.

"Why the fuck did they send six of you?" I hear from behind me with a poke.

I turn and looking down at me is a marine easily a foot taller and about twice as wide.

"Fuck if I know. Why did they send so many marines?" I ask back.

At this the marines snickers, "Fuck if I know" he laughs back.

The eggs are in a cube, the bacon is fake, something is clearly wrong with the food printer, my bread has actual layer shift lines. I shake my head, despite all the improvements in star ships and hyper sleep chambers, apparently the auto cook was low on their priorities.

I take my place in the center of the table, a spot left for me by Janice. "Five to One" Marco mumbles. "They send thirty marines and six cyber techs."

"More than that." replies Janice. "They only opened half the pods. It's ten to one."

"Not quite. I looked at the roster, there's a bunch of scientists with us as well. But Marco is right, they did bring quite a few marines." I reply.

"Why so many?" Marco asks. He's asking me because I'm technically considered the senior officer. Technically because the Colonial Marines in their disdain basically excluded the cybernetics techs out of their usual command chain. Cybernetics technician first class is all the rank I'll ever get, unless I want to head off to "real" boot camp.

The fact we get to even call ourselves marines pisses off the regular marines. All the rigger, all the physical training, all the crawling in mud, zero g combat stuff they do, we don't. Instead it's lots of invasive surgery for neural shunts, brain augmentation devices and lots and lots of studying.

"Are they planning on going down?" asks Ruvi, yet another long standing member of our cyber tech squad. All these questions I don't have answers to. "I know only a tiny bit more than you guys do. I got roughly the same orders you got, showed up to the same packaging facility and found myself here." They know I know more, but realizing that I'm not going to divulge more details in present company, everybody gets back to the business of eating their slop. The other three members of the squad having asked no questions were still in the shell shock phase of this whole thing. I pat Jeremy on the shoulder. "How you handling your first trip as a cyber so far?" I ask. "Better than I thought. Damn neural shunt is itching like hell though." I get up and look at his neck. It's red as hell. He was the only member of our team who was a former Colonial Marine, in some ways it pisses the other marines off even more. "After muster, go find Dickinson and have her look at that."  He simply nods. I know
hey won't. He'll suffer in silence.


Chapter 2.


We line up on our spot on the muster line. 

The marines line up ramrod straight.

"Company at attention!" yells a sergeant.

"Attend hut!" the marines yell back, echoing through the cavernous chamber. Our group manages to bellow out an "Attend hut" with the rest of them. The sergeant walks the line. Stopping at Janice.

"Is that scarf regulation Ms. Allman?" snaps the sergeant.

"No sir." she replies.

"Loose it!" he replies, shaking his head and moving down the line.

At this a couple of marines snort. A loose button, a couple BDU's not cuffed properly. He's a real stickler for detail. "Ready Room one, on the double!" yells the sergeant. Now happy that in the case of sudden depressurization of the hull or fire, at least the forty odd people's corpses will look good or something like that.

Being last in line, the room had exactly thirty seats. We all know our spot on the line and the number of seats is without accident. We dutifully stand in the back. The usual mild hazing, the norm.

Seeing this, the sergeant snaps, "Get our techs some seats!"

At this a marine out loud jokes, "Why, all they do is sit anyway?"

"Stow it Wikowsky and now you're getting them seats!" the sergeant barks back. From a recess on the wall, additional flat pack desk chairs are unpacked and handed out. Satisfied everybody now has seats, the CO strides in. The marines all stand extra straight. Looking everybody over, he simply
nods. "At ease, let's get started." We all take our seats.

Robinson is as classic a commanding officer of the Colonial Marines as one could make. Crisp uniform, polished boots, name tag centered perfectly.

A wall screen starts up showing a planet of swirl of muddled oranges, browns and whites. Below it are bold letters that read "LV426". Head nods and fist bumps ripple through the Marines.

"Welcome back to the shit hole known as LV426. Also know as Acheron, Hadley's Hope and a few other wilder and colorful names. We all know this is also the first planet where we encountered what is now know as the Xenomorph." A murmur moves through the room. A couple marines shake their fists, a Xenomorph head is tattooed on the back of their hands.

"Our job here is to recon the planet to evaluate it for habitability and additional threats. Even though the primary atmospheric processor that was installed near the settlement was destroyed, the four additional automated atmospheric processors have continued to function. Wayland has spend considerable money on this planet and they've hired our outfit to ensure that there is nothing left of the previous tenants."

So, this is a corporate gig, it makes sense. Why abandon a perfectly serviceable planet just because it had had a few pesky inhabitants?

At this a marines hand shot up, "Yes Corporal?"

"What if we find more hostiles?" the corporal asked.

"Recon first, hostile elimination if needed." replies the CO.

I knew this was coming. I'd been given a prior briefing explaining some
of the mission parameters.

The moment we came out of hyperspace, we began mapping the surface.
The screen changes to show the planet without the clouds. A quick zoom in shows the crater where the primary atmospheric processor and the settlement had been.
The other atmospheric processors are highlighted on the map. As well as a red zone. "This is the location of the derelict alien ship. It is known to contain Xenomorph eggs. It is considered a no-go
zone. Our objectives are the processors. In particular processors two and three that has gone off the grid."

The screen turns off.

"Cyber will be landing Rockies first, then if deemed needed, marines." Hands slapped desks, general groans, more than a couple marines looked back and gave us looks I'm sure their grandmothers would have disapproved of.

Since the original events on LV426, the Xenomorph had become a cultural boogie man for the Colonial Marines. More than a few marines in this group sport Xenomorph tattoos and talk righteously of avenging their fallen brothers. Here they are, at the place it had first gone down and the prospect of unleashing hell on their enemies was being snatched from them. Jeremy had told me that a big part of their training now consisted of simulated combat with Xenomorphs. Guys build tiny shrines depicting
their eventual victories over this foe. Xenomorph heads as trophies are a popular thing to come out of the various printers. I wasn't unsympathetic to their feelings or cause. Though to me, going nose to
nose with a predator that could at best kill me or at worst use me as a womb for another Xenomorph doesn't sound appealing at all. As far as I am concerned, I'm more than happy to merely orbit the planet. Landing in ankle deep mud and fighting monsters is a job for robots and these guys.

However, the events on LV426 had lead to me being here. After the debacle, the military realized that threats like the Xenomorphs weren't going to be easily tackled by men and women in body armor. Two major innovations happened that changed things in a big way. The first was the subspace radio. This allowed for far faster comms across stellar distances and near instantaneous communications within a single solar system, taking control and sensing to the next level. The second innovation was the creation of neural shunt technology that allows for the true blending of man and machine. Suddenly the
military was presented with the reality of having robots they could control at great distances. You might be asking why even come on this ship then? As the Astro techs have tried to explain some of the math, the farther you are away, the lower the amount of bandwidth. Communicating with Earth is now so slow it can be measured in bits per minute. As this session dragged on and various training and maintenance tasks were handed out a few marines groused more and more about not getting down to the planet, I wondered why they'd even been thawed. A thought a couple marines had expressed as well. Others took it in stride. Fine if they weren't going to get down to the planet, they were going to be riding our asses and looking at every pixel we fed back.

This whole thing was going to be hot garbage anyway you cut it.

With the dog and pony show concluded, Robinson strides out the ready room, ramrod straight with his admin in tow.

"Deployment will be at 0700 tomorrow. Cybers prep for drop. Dismissed!" barked the Sargent.

Walking back to "Candy land" as everybody called our module the grumbles in the halls get louder.

Wikowsky pushes his way up to our group, stabbing a finger in my chest, "All this bullshit so you weenies can shuffle around in your tin cans?"

I've been to this rodeo plenty of times. The trick was to show the guy think you're in the same boat as him and then give him a carrot. "Look man, I'm just following orders like the rest of you. If we find something, you'll see it as well. The feeds will be going to everybody."

"The Mark I eyeball is all I need." he replies which brings out a couple "yeas" from the group that has formed around us. I could sense the tension in the air, this guy had a Xenomorph tattoo on his forearm. "We have chain of command for a reason. Talk to your CO, I've got orders."

Jeremy's hand catches Wikowsky's fist millimeters from my face, he then spins into a jujitsu move that has Wikowsky pinned to the wall. Nose to nose, Jeremy growls, "Stow it. We've got enough going on without your bullshit as well." At this, Jeremy drops the lock and gave the guy a
good shove. We turn and walk away. "Keep walking" he yells.

After a couple more paces he finishes with "He's a traitor to the Corps and you're not even fucking marines. Why they let you chicken shits on this mission I'll never know."

Sadly, this isn't the first time I've dealt with this. I simply point to the corner and say, "Tsk tsk, Cameras."

Realizing that like every part of the ship, this is being recorded, he finally gets the hint, turns heel and stomps off.

"What a fucking hot head" quips Janice.

"Seriously. How did he get through the damn pysch eval?" jokes
Marco. Jeremy snickers, "You have no idea what they look for in an marine,
by their standards, he's perfect." 
Marco and Ruvi just shake their heads.


Chapter 3.


0700 gives us nearly ten hours to do final load out on our rockies. The real name is some word salad of "Remotely Operational Cyber Kenetic Enabled Recon Systems" or some shit. Everybody just called them rockies in an homage to the old film about a boxer and the rock'em sockem robots game
that someone had dutifully printed out and affixed to the wall of the prep room.

The other funnier name that went around was "killer mushrooms" due to the large sensor dome that affords the operator a 360 degree view of their surroundings. On four articulated legs and with two powerful and two smaller manipulator arms, they could damn near climb anything, cut through anything and come home to talk about it. Covered in high tech ceramic armor and weapon hard points they could take a beating and also bring a substantial amount of firepower to a situation as well.

For this mission we'd also been issued "sleds." Drop vehicles that contain their own power supplies and can carry a rocky great distances as well as carry spares and additional ammo. Cradled in their storage units, we looked at the load out manifests. "What the fuck is this shit?" quips Jeremy.  I came over to look at his tablet. 

"Glue dispensers?" pointing to the tablet. I took a deep breath, time for the rest of the briefing. "Everybody take a seat." I said. "The marines got told what they needed, we have secondary orders to capture a Xenomorph if possible. Hence the aforementioned glue dispensers."

"And then do what? Bring it on this ship? No fucking way." replies Janice.
"According to the brass they've got some magic resin to encase and rendered them inert." I explain. "Jesus have mercy on us." mumbles Marco. The last member of our group was a petite woman named Roberta, but goes by the handle Roxxie. "Roxxie, You've been quiet through all this. Feel
free to speak up." At this, she let out a sigh. "A missions a mission, but this seems like a really bad one. I've read the files on what those things are capable of. Acid for blood, super strong, immune to vacuum. 
Has one even been captured to test this containment on?" 

We all know the answer to that. Even one had been, we'd have been the last to be told about it. "I know saying trust the system is clique here, but in this case, we're going to have to roll with it." Everybody nods. We all know the score. There were bigger things afoot here, we just weren't entirely privy to all the details. "Look on the bright side, we've got latitude on our load outs. Pack for a party!" Like kids in a candy store, they go to work. Ammo drums are hosted into place, bolts tightened, connectors checked. Over the next three hours drop details are fed to my tablet. 

Ruvi pings me. "Do we even know the drop point?" he asks. In response, I forward the entire intel packet to everybody on the team. "Sir, you just gave me everything." replies Ruvi. "Yes. I've got that power and I'm using it. Let's not go in with eyes half open. Two teams. Team one is Myself, Marco and Janice. Team two is Ruvi, Roxxie and Jeremy."

I wanted to make sure there was someone senior on each team. Roxxie has a cool head and is a solid respected leader in the group. More data flows in. My team is going to investigate why atmospheric processor two isn't calling home. Team two is going to investigate the third processor plant. Everybody nods happily with this arrangement. "Let's get some rest, at up at oh four thirty and by oh five hundred, I expect everybody ready to enter their cradles and jack in."

Chapter 4.


Dinner is somewhat nicer than our wake up meal. Apparently a tech somewhere had tinkered with the auto cook because I get something that resembles an actual hamburger. Not ones to complain, we eat our soy protein burgers, head back and hit the sack. We get the usual side eye and head shakes but we're left unmolested otherwise.

Four thirty is damn early, anyway you cut it. The marines make you used to it, but it's still rough. I get up, bang on doors and got sundry mumbles. Roxxie and Ruvi appear to be rooming together, honestly, I not surprised and I'm happy for them.

Janice strides out of her quarters, haptic suit on and ready to roll. The two of us exchange knowing glances and Janice glances at Roxxie's door and simply raises her eyebrows. I shrug back. Frankly, I was hoping for a late night knock that didn't come.

As Janice and I are warm up our cradles, the other members stroll in, hot coffee in hand and climb down into their cradles and start the warm up procedures. For cybernetics technicians, jacking in has a
number of serious dangers. The most common to newer users is known as transition shock. Your brain is quite happy with all the usual sensory input and then you pull the rug out and give it different and additional inputs. Our group at this point has been jacking in at the low end for two years and at the high end like myself for nearly a decade. I slide my head back into the cradle and feel the click of the interface to my neural shunt. My head is now locked in place, the visor slides down and I feel the various cinch points as the haptic suit pins me down.

"Initiate transition" I say. Immediately I'm dropped into darkness.
"Commander, unit number one is ready." I say "Thank you Alice" but my lips don't move. I'm fully shunted in. Alice is the AI for our group. We're encouraged to give them names and interact with them as full team members.  There's so much data flowing in, without an AI to help collate it, you'd quickly be overwhelmed. "Status check" I say into the comms channel.  "Team 1 ready, team 2 ready." I get firm replies. Good, everybody has jacked in without issue. I switch over to the command channel.  "Cybertech teams  one and two ready." I say into the channel.  "Roger that. Hold tight, we are adjusting our orbit for optimal insertion."  a voice says back. Into the channel I've designated
"Team 1".  The operational CO speaks up. "Team 1, prepare for drop in thirty seconds.  Team 2, drop in 45 seconds."

This part is a bit of a nothing burger for us. The sleds and our rockies will either make it to the ground in one piece or they simply drop another unit. We have six units, plus an additional six ready to go and just as many in spares that can be assembled. During this time we access the various optical systems on the nadir side of the ship. Janice pings me. "Glad we've got subspace on the rockys, it looks like one hell of storm in the north."  I change my perspective and she is right. While always harsh, the detonation of the primary processor had thrown gigatons of dust into the atmosphere, helping to trap more heat and ironically speeding up the terraforming process.  A wet and rainy planet now with extra heat pumped into it, meant big storms.

"I wonder if the issue with unit three is water intrusion or a bolt of lightning?" remarks Marco into our group channel. Privately I hoped, though these stations were built to be dropped from orbit and "just work."

Bingo, five by five, confirmation of soft landing. The petals of the drop capsule unfold. The sleds treads lock down. All green. Already the sensors on my rocky were registering a gust of 50km/h.

Alice shifts through the spectrum's, nothing. Without needing any urging we proceeded towards the processing plant. Fuck. The blast doors are ajar, at least a half meter. 
"I was really hoping to find nobody home." Janice whispers into the chat. 
"Me too." I whisper back.

I notice that Alice had already brought all the weapons online.  Alice and I had been paired more than five years ago, it had gotten some intuition about it and I appreciated it. "No damage to the doors" Alice notes. Maybe just a mechanical screw up? Marco creeps forward, deploying his smaller set of dexterous hands he manipulates the controls. The door grinds open a couple more centimeters. "That's all I'm going to get, the mechanism is seized or jammed, I can't tell from here." Not surprising, water
likely got into the track.

I move forward and look closer. I unlimbered my big arms. I attempted to pry the doors, but they only start to deform. Time to ask to break stuff. "Permission to cut into the blast doors?" I ask. "Granted. Breach the doors and enter."  the CO orders.

My plasma torch makes quick work of the blast doors. Plates of durasteel fall to the mud with a sizzle. All three of us stalk through the steaming opening into the lower entry bay of atmospheric processor number three.

"Fuck me." whispers Marco. Behind me I can see that the door mechanism is gummed up with Alien excretion. Every wall surface and the ceiling are caked with it. Frozen into the walls are various shapes, their chests burst open, but so coated in excretion and decayed as to make them unrecognizable. "Confirm hostiles" I said into the channel.
"Roger that. Team two confirms evidence of hostiles as well." comes through on the command channel. "I don't understand, there's nothing on this rock for them to eat or use as wombs?" Marco gestures at the wall.
"Eh, please don't say womb." replies Janice. I can feel her disgust in the channel. The dangers of being jacked in, bits of emotion travel across the link. "Sorry." replies Marco. "Get samples" comes through the channel. "Roger." I bark back. A compartment in the front of my rocky opens, I dutifully scrap some of the alien excretions into the container, It snaps shut. I can tell the fusion reactor is online, the vibration is enough I can feel it through the haptic suit.

"Deploy recon drones" I order. Triangular drones lift off from the top of each of our sensor stacks. "Marco, Up, Janice down, I'll take straight ahead." I call out. 
This is for the benefit of the marines I know are watching the feed. Alice controls the drones as they fly through the facility. Doppler pulses, wide band IR and optical scanners feed information back to Alice and into my mind mapping out the facility and comparing it to schematics. I locate the nearest network interface and patch in. The fusion reactor is functioning but at greatly reduced power. Its heat exchangers have been compromised. Enough power to keep things barely operational but not enough to run comms. This smells exactly like the reports from the other facility. 

"Contact." Janice announces into the channel. "Lower level, My drone got swatted out of the air. I have four distinct entities moving."
"Xenomorphs?" I ask. "It happened fast sir, the drone was nailed from above." A quick reply shows the drone be swatted by the dark, vaguely biomechancial hand of a Xeno. 
"Deploy another?" she asks. 
"Save them. They know we're here.” I bring the camera feeds up. Dozens of cameras and only
7 were operational? The operational ones have been encased, their feeds useless. It seems the Xenomorphs intentionally covered them or destroyed them, but why would they know or care? Yet another question to file away. My drone returns. Nobody home on the ground level.

At once all our trackers start pinging. 
"Movement, upper level, and lower." Marco calls out. 
I heard the tromping of feet on steel treads from above. These remote processors weren't that
elaborate. Three levels, Just a fusion reactor, large vents to draw in air, machinery to split out the bad stuff, add in the good stuff and shoot the atmosphere out. No control room, just a single console, spare
parts storage and miles of pipe and cables.

A dozen drones spill out of the stairway. Some are full size drones we've all seen in the archive footage, others are slightly smaller, thinner, pale Xenos with weirdly curved heads. Training fully kicks in,
I track and fired a three round burst through the closest drone. Configured to burst in the chest cavity, the first drone blows in half throwing green acidic blood and guts in every direction. Immediately
a stripe of optics goes out, Automatically the sensor rotates to put the dark spot at the back. 

Janice nails one of the pale smaller Xenomorphs with the glue canister.  A thick mesh of adhesive has entangled the creature. Identical to it's larger cousin, it struggles and snarls as the glue sets. "Not sure how it would work against a big one, but we bagged ourselves a mini." Marco, our resident nerd about all things Xenomorph chimes in, "None of the intel I've ever seen has shown one this small, or skinny." Small is relative. This creature is still near two meters tall, just very thin and pale, with an oddly shaped head. Drones keep stalking forward now unsure what to do with these new foes. I order our team to back out of the processor. Acid eating through some critical part of the cooling system of the fusion
reactor is definitely a "bad thing." I'm also keenly aware a 10mm caseless HE round in the wrong spot will quickly end this mission. The drones surge through the gap we'd cut in the door, only to be
shredded three strides out. The assault ends quickly. Alice has kept a running count, eleven in total. Janice creeps back inside and retrieves our prize. Marco is busy sampling the various fluids
leaking from the drones. 
"This ain't good" I hear over the channel.
"I've got a jar made out of their super material, it held the Xeno blood for about five minutes." Looking over I see him holding up the jar, the bottom of it gone.

"Team one, return to dust off point with the specimen." At this Janice opens a Private channel. "This is bullshit. Marco just showed them that their magic test jar couldn't stop their blood. Do you want to be on the DeBe with that thing?" The honest answer was no.
"Command, this is Team 1 leader. Reporting that Marco's containment vessel failed. Requesting sit eval on return of specimen to DeBraso?"

"We are aware of the situation" comes the reply. It’s easy to forget that all our video is being distributed across the ship into various war rooms. Minutes go by as we wait. Privately in our channel
we muse about conversations being had on the bridge.

"Orders are as follows. Restrain specimen to Marco's sled. Investigate transmitted grid location." Marco immediately goes to his sled and unloads additional ammo canisters and a spare portable reactor.
Janice pipes up in our side channel, "What the fuck is the plan?"
"Dunno. Follow orders, go to the grid location." I said flatly.
We secure the Xeno to Marco's sled as it hisses and snaps at us. As soon as the last tie down is cinched, it promptly trundles off over the horizon. We stowed ourselves and the supplies onto the remaining sleds. 
"That little one was weird, what was up with its head?" asks Marco. 
Both Janice and I say nothing. This whole thing is weird and getting weirder. The sleds grind through the mud leaving deep furrows as they chug towards our next objective.
"Alice, please eject me." I said to the AI. 
Immediately my senses reverted back to the usual's. I hit the eject button and the visor slides back and I felt the neural shunt disengage. Janice and Marco stepped up out of the pits as well. 
"Estimated travel time for the sleds is two hours." I say. They nod. 
There were long term effects of the neural shunts. Headaches being the most prominent one. I could feel one brewing and in more than one way. I jogged slowly down one of the central hab spars that ran the length of the ship. To get the blood flowing and to get some answers. Soon I find myself at the
Combat Information Center (CIC) door. 
"Permission to Enter?" I asked at the intercom. 
The door slids open. "Permission granted." I got gruffly back. On the walls I could see the six views
from the rocky's as they roll over the terrain. 
A tech turns and says over her shoulder, "Team three drop ship is nominal. ETA nine minutes."

Wait what, I reeled a bit. Another team? We are the only cybers on the ship? That can only mean one thing. 
Commander Robinson clears his throat, "As you are well aware PFC Hurgs, we are the middle of a
mission. Other than stretching your legs, why are you in my CIC?"
"Sir, I'm concerned about the failure of Marco's test vessel."
At this he simply shakes his head. "Son, you are well above your pay grade here. That's not the only vessel we brought with us. We've got the situation handled. Get back in your pit and get back to
the mission." I was then tossed out of the CIC.

This whole thing stinks. I deeply dislike being used as a cog in a machine I didn't know what direction is traveling. I walk back to candy land and it hits me. Dr. Jaggo. I climbed back into the pit, but I don't jack in. Instead I write up a quick note to one of the techs back on Earth. IF anybody had been involved in the creation of the storage vessels, it is Dr. Jaggo. Normally our paths wouldn't have crossed except
for that she'd been pulled into a project to outfit the rocky's with composite armor to make the more resistant to the creatures wildly acidic blood. She is the expert when it comes to the Xenos blood. The message sits in the send queue. Weird. I checked the logs. Subspace link offline? Yet another weird thing. Janice and Marco were already jacked back in, I follow suit.

What a rainy nasty planet. The storm we'd seen from orbit had spun off a series of smaller storms and we are enjoying the benefits of one of them. Wind lashs the sleds. We tumble through the various bands
looking for anything alive. Around us the ground is glowing with lichen mats, the puddles teem with microbial life. 
"Weird wildlife" Marco said into the channel to no one in particular.
"I don't understand how there were drones. There's supposed to be no large life forms on this planet." replies Janice. 
It's true. This was something that had bothered us and had become hotly debated while our robots traversed the plant.  The Xeno life cycle was well known.
"What was encased on the walls then?" I ask.
To that there's simply silence in the channel. We turn our attention to the planet. The landscape is windswept and desolate. The planet is beautiful in a primordial way. I can see how eventually with decades of terra-forming it will eventually be nice. Get some very hardy GMO grasses going, calm down the atmosphere. I let the sensor data wash over me as muse about this. This place could be a paradise one day, but that was far in the future. It was Marco who saw it first. "What the fuck is that?"
he said into the channel.

I snap out of my daydream about the planet and realized he is talking about the Xenos. Marco's rocky springs from the sled and lands agile like a cat. 
"Is that a pig?" asked Janice. 
Alice answers into the chat, "Based on image analysis, yes." 
The three rocky's scan around this pigs corpse. It's chest blown open in classic Xenomorph form. "It's not decayed much if not at all." I notice. "CIC, you seeing this?" I say into the command channel. Over the channel I heard commander Robinson's exhale. 
"We see it. This might change the number of hostiles." 
In our private channel Marco muttered, "Ya think!" Marco climbs back aboard and we trundle on, however now all three of us fully extended our sensor bulbs. Without prompting Marco launches an additional drones to provide a larger swath of over watch, yet again showing initiative.



Chapter 5.


"What an ugly hole in the ground." says Marco. Both Janice and I acknowledge it in the channel, it was. Three meters high and seven meters wide, it descends down at a steep angle. 
"Permission to launch a drone into Hadley's asshole?" asks Marco.
